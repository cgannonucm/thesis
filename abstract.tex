\begin{abstract} 
 Dark matter is thought to comprise the majority of the matter budget of the universe and serves a critical role in the history of our universe: baryons and dark matter collapse together, believed to be primordial fluctuations in the density field. 
 Despite its important role in the evolution of our universe, its nature remains elusive.
 Large scale cosmological probes such as Planck, measurements of the spatial and mass functions of luminous galaxies the relative positions of dark matter and gas in the bullet cluster have provided evidence for cold and collisionless dark matter (CDM) on the largest scale of the universe.  
 
 Over the last few decades, new methods of probing smaller scale structures have emerged allowing CDM theories to be tested on the sub-galactic scale.
 Gravitational lensing is a particularly exciting avenue for probing this regime: it allows dark matter structure to be probed without the need for luminous tracers . 
 Improved understanding of the predictions of dark matter models are needed to compliment the advance of observational probes to the sub-galactic scale. 
 A significant challenge on this front is the simulation of subhalos, which are halos evolving within a tidal field of a larger galaxy. Subhalos present a unique challenge as they often undergo significant mass loss, drastically increasing the resolution necessary to resolve them in N-body simulations. 
 In this work, we study numerical predictions from analytic, Semi-analytic and N-body simulations for dark matter subhalos of galaxy lens scale halos in both CDM and alternate dark matter models. We develop informed priors on various population level quantities to support lensing studies of dark matter.
 This work is split into two main sections: first, we develop informed priors on the normalization of the halo mass function using semi-analytic models, second, we develop a new computationally efficient method for simulating SIDM subhalos in 1-D. 

 Many alternate dark matter models (such as warm dark matter or WDM) predict a suppression in the halo mass function, which is the differential number of halos per unit mass. In these models, there is a mass scale below which the number of halos is significantly reduced.
 Recent work has drawn attention attention to artificial disruption of subhalos in CDM N-body simulations, which may artificially lower predictions of the normalization of the subhalo mass function.  
 To account for this theoretical uncertainty, lensing studies of dark matter have previously adopted wide priors on this normalization; however, this creates degeneracy when constraining properties of dark matter models such as any potential turnover in the halo mass function. 
 To estimate current theoretical uncertainty in the subhalo mass function, we use the state-of-the-art semi-analytic \gal model tuned to ultra high resolution N-body simulations that minimize the effects of artificial disruption.
 Using this model we develop informed priors on the projected halo mass function expected by lensing studies, develop models for the scaling of the halo mass function with redshift and halo mass and explore the tidal effects of a realistically evolving potential of a central galaxy. 
 We find that a factor of $\sim 2$ accounts for theoretical uncertainties in the subhalos mass function and find that for current lensing studies the central galaxy has a currently negligible impact on the projected number density of substructure.  
 
 Recent inferences on the sub-galactic scale from direct gravitational imaging have found higher than predicted inner densities of dark matter halos. 
 A explanations is a class of alternate dark matter models with non-negligible self-interaction on the sub-galactic scales, called self-interacting dark matter (SIDM); SIDM models with a sufficiently high cross section on the subgalactic scale can produce highly dense halos due to gravothermal collapse. 
 3-D N-body simulations, which are traditionally the primary method of studying dark matter halos are computationally expensive especially in the core collapse regime; tidal mass loss amplifies this challenge by requiring a larger number of particles to resolve the halo initially, often requiring tens of thousands of core hours to properly resolve a single core collapse even in idealized simulations. 
 In this work, we develop a pipeline to simulate the evolution of idealized SIDM subhalos in 1-D with realistic tidal evolution; by simulating subhalos evolution in 1-D, we are able to simulate SIDM physics much more computationally efficiently than the 3D case, resulting in over an order of magnitude speed up in some case enabling previously infeasible exploration of SIDM model space. 
 Using our models, we explore the core collapse rates for a wide range for a wide range of SIDM cross sections for a realistic population of halos. 
 We find that halos up to $\sim 4$ times as many halos collapse as subhalos when compared to identical at infall field halos if evaporation is negligible.  
 
\end{abstract}
